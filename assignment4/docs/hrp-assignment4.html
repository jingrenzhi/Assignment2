Results

I fit a linear regression model (lm function) to evaluate how various factors affect the healthcare costs.

model <- lm(
  cost ~ smoke + female + age  + cardiac,   # linear regression 
  data = data
)

The linear regression results shown above report that all four covariates (smoking status, gender, age, and cardiac condition) are significantly associated with healthcare costs (p < 0.001 for all covariates). Specifically, being a smoker is associated with an increase of approximately $542 in healthcare costs, while being female is associated with a reduction of about $253. Each additional year of age increases cost by roughly $15.78, and having a cardiac condition is associated to an increase of about $408. The model has a R-square of 0.7551, which means that the model explains approximately 75.5% of the variation in healthcare costs.

Based on the fitted model, we can generate predicted healthcare costs and examine the residuals to assess model performance. The plot below shows healthcare costs versus the predicted costs, with the red dashed line representing the line \(y=x\), which indicates perfect prediction. Most of the data points are near the line, suggesting the model performs reasonably well overall.

Discussion

In this analysis, I examine the relationship between healthcare costs and key influencing factors, and find that costs are significantly associated with individual characteristics such as smoking status, gender, age, and the presence of cardiac conditions. Due to privacy concerns, the analysis is conducted using simulated data; however, all key steps are fully reproducible and can be applied to real-world datasets. Based on the simulated data, the results show that all predictors in the model are statistically significant, and the full model explains over 75% of the variation in healthcare costs.

The residual plot indicates that the linear model is appropriate, with residuals generally centered around zero and no evident patterns or heteroscedasticity. The actual vs. predicted cost plot further supports the model’s accuracy, as most observations fall close to the line of perfect prediction. However, a few outliers exhibit residuals as large as $500, which may be due to unmeasured factors (such as socioeconomic status or the presence of other medical conditions) not captured in the simulated data.
